## Tour Overview

For 41 years, Czechoslovakia lived on the eastern side of the Iron Curtain. Once a modern, cosmopolitan democracy, it became a country where your job, your children's school and your holidays depended on keeping quiet. Then, in November 1989, it all came apart in a matter of days.

I was born in 1989. I was a baby on 17 November, when students marched down Národní třída and the Velvet Revolution began. I grew up in the country that came after, surrounded by people who remembered everything before it.

This tour isn't a list of dates, and it isn't a lecture on how evil the regime was. It's about people: those who believed, those who went along to get by, and those who refused and paid for it. No cartoon villains, just the honest story of how ordinary families lived through extraordinary times.

### Topics covered

- The post-war mood, and why so many Czechs voted Communist in 1946
- The Communist coup of February 1948
- The political trials of the 1950s
- The Prague Spring and the 1968 Warsaw Pact invasion
- Normalisation and daily life: queues, weekend cottages and quiet compromises
- Charter 77, the dissidents and Václav Havel
- The Velvet Revolution, and what came after
