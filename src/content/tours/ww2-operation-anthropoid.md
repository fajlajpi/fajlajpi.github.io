## Tour Overview

Its strong industry, long German heritage and strategic position made Czechoslovakia irresistible to Hitler. It was handed over at Munich in 1938 and occupied in March 1939. Prague became the capital of the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia, and its people were subjugated and bent, but never broken.

My great-grandfather fought in the First World War as a Czechoslovak legionnaire, one of the men who helped bring this country into being in 1918. So this tour starts there: with what the young republic was, what it lost, and why. From there we follow the story through the occupation and Operation Anthropoid, the boldest act of resistance in occupied Europe, all the way to liberation and the uneasy years that led to 1948.

### Sites visited

- The Legion Bridge (Most Legií), named after the Czechoslovak Legions
- The National Theatre
- The Dancing House, built on a site bombed in 1945
- Church of Saints Cyril and Methodius, and the crypt where the paratroopers made their last stand
- Places connected to the Heydrich assassination
- Emmaus Monastery, hit in the bombing of February 1945

### Topics covered

- The First Republic and the Czechoslovak Legions
- The Munich Agreement of 1938 and the invasion of 1939
- Life in the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia
- The Holocaust in the Czech lands
- Reinhard Heydrich and Operation Anthropoid
- Lidice, the reprisals and the resistance
- The Prague Uprising and the liberation
- The post-war turn towards Communism
